# The sophon on the 404 — 2026-09-10

**Scope:** #11, on `main...droplet-404`. The page that isn't there.
**Harness:** unchanged at 43/43 — this touches no simulation code.

The smallest ticket on the list. Three of its premises had gone stale, it took
nine review findings, and then the subject changed.

## The subject changed, late and on purpose

The ticket asked for a droplet. Most of this branch's history is a droplet: a
renderer, a page, and a review round that found the highlights were nailed to
the page while the file claimed the opposite.

It is a sophon now, proposed after all of that, and it is the better object for
this page:

- A **droplet** is about annihilation. It arrives beautiful, says *we come in
  peace*, and takes a fleet apart. Apt for a lot of things; it is about
  violence.
- A **sophon** is about a locked door. Two unfolded protons sit inside our
  accelerators making the results lie, so every experiment comes back nonsense
  and physics stops. Not destruction — a guarantee that you will not find what
  you are looking for, however many times you ask.

That is what a 404 *is*. "Something got here first" is true of a sophon and true
of a dead URL, and the line under it still tells a visitor who has never read
the books exactly what happened.

**The droplet work is not lost, and was not wasted.** #10 is the droplet cursor,
and that renderer is its consumer — proven, colour-safe, and reviewed. It is
lifted out of this branch rather than shipped with no caller, which is what it
would have been the moment the 404 stopped drawing one:

```
git show fd7bb11:src/lib/droplet.ts
git show fd7bb11:src/components/DropletCanvas.tsx
```

Its nine findings are kept below, because every one of them is about code #10
will pick up.

## What the ticket assumed

**`水滴` was no longer in the font subset.** The ticket says *"no font-subset
change needed."* `水` was there; **`滴` was not** — it was one of the twenty
orphans #12 removed when `check:glyphs` was repaired. Because #12 also made that
check *enforce*, this was a red build rather than a silent fallback:

```
1 glyph(s) used but missing from CJK_GLYPHS: 滴   exit 1
```

The subject change then took `水滴` back out of the copy, so `滴` is orphaned
again — and the same check caught *that*, the other way round. `智` and `子` went
in for `智子`, both of which were also among the twenty. The subset is 79. Three
separate catches from one repair, in two days.

**There was no droplet renderer to reuse.** `SystemCanvas` draws suns, worlds and
trails and nothing else, and #10 has not been built. The dependency ran the other
way, which is what makes the lift-out above cheap.

**The 404 already runs the simulation.** See below.

## The sophon

`src/lib/sophon.ts` draws one thing: the unfolding. A sophon is proton-sized and
therefore invisible, so everything memorable about it is a symptom — the
countdown in Wang Miao's vision, the CMB flickering, the sky opening. The sky
opening is the only one that is an object rather than an effect, and the only
one that can be drawn without either a photosensitivity hazard or a ticking
number that reads as *something is expiring*.

It takes an `unfold` in 0..1 and owns no clock. At 0 it is a proton: one speck
of light and nothing else on the page. At 1 it is a surface etched over every
inch with circuitry, held at a slight tilt so it reads as curved rather than as
a target.

**The etching had to be louder than the wireframe.** The first pass had latitude
and longitude lines at 0.16 and the circuit traces at 0.07, and it read as a
wireframe globe — a network icon, not an unfolded proton. Rebalanced to 0.14 for
the traces against 0.11 and 0.09 for the mesh, with fewer of both, it reads as
something printed. Judged on screen; there is no measurement for this.

## The timing, which is most of the design

`SophonCanvas` holds a 46-second cycle: **17 seconds folded**, 6.5 opening, 12
held, 5 closing, and folded again. So the sphere is fully open for 26% of the
cycle and on screen at all for 51%, and for the other 49% the page carries one
speck of light and nothing else. (This paragraph said "about two thirds" until
finding 7 below; the timing is called most of the design, so its numbers are
load-bearing.)

A visitor who arrives from a dead link and leaves in five seconds will almost
certainly see nothing move. **That is correct.** A loop short enough to
guarantee they catch it turns a rare event into an animation playing at them,
which is the failure mode of every decorative background ever shipped — and it
is the same trap the droplet version fell into from the other side, where the
fix for "too static" was to add a second motion.

Reduced motion renders one frame **held open** rather than at t = 0. The
unfolding is the whole idea, and a still page showing one dot is
indistinguishable from a bug.

## The constraint that could not be met, and what the number does and does not settle

The ticket says the 404 *"needs a droplet and three notional light positions, not
`EraProvider` and a running three-body integrator. Keep the page cheap."*

`EraProvider` wraps `{children}` in the **root layout**, so it wraps
`/_not-found` exactly as it wraps every route, and its loop advances the
integrator whether or not a canvas has registered — deliberately, since the
counter has to keep counting and `--heat` has to keep driving the palette on
`/about` too. Nothing this page does can change that.

Measured instead — one second of simulation, with nothing drawing it:

```
5.40 ms per second of wall clock   (median of 12)
0.090 ms per frame                 against a 16.7 ms budget
0.54% of one core
```

**Accepted, and the constraint amended** — on narrower grounds than first
claimed. That figure covers the integrator's own cost, on one machine, with
nothing drawing it: it excludes the per-frame `--heat` write and the style
invalidation that follows, and it predates this page's own animation loop.

The alternatives are still worse — a route group outside the provider costs the
404 its heat wash and its footer counter and splits the layout for one page, and
skipping integration when nothing draws would stop the counter advancing on
`/about`, which is the visitor's own history. But *keep the page cheap* on a page
nobody stays on is about wake-ups and battery rather than CPU share, and no
figure here settles that. What was done instead of arguing it: the canvas stops
on `visibilitychange`, so a hidden 404 wakes the main thread for neither loop.
The wake-up question is open and wants a profiler on the route.

## Not verified by driving

**Anything under real `requestAnimationFrame`.** The preview pane reports
`document.hidden` and delivers no animation frames, so every motion test ran
with a timer standing in, and at a cycle several times faster than the shipped
one. Nobody has watched a 17-second wait end in the sky opening, which is the
entire experience of the page.

**Whether the wait is right.** 17 seconds folded is a judgement about how rare a
surprise should be on a page most people leave in five. It cannot be measured;
it can only be sat through.

# The droplet, and its review — kept for #10

Nine findings against the droplet renderer, which now moves to the cursor
ticket. Left here in full because every one is about code #10 inherits, and the
first is worth reading before touching a rotating canvas ever again.

## 1. The highlights did not move. The rotation cancelled itself — fixed

```ts
const local = light.angle - rotation; // inside a ctx already rotated by rotation
```

Composing the two gives `light.angle` exactly. Verified numerically — the screen
offset for one light across five rotations:

```
rot 0.00 -> screen offset 0.360534 -0.454330
rot 0.50 -> screen offset 0.360534 -0.454330
rot 1.70 -> screen offset 0.360534 -0.454330
rot 3.14 -> screen offset 0.360534 -0.454330
rot 5.00 -> screen offset 0.360534 -0.454330
```

Identical to six decimals. The three suns did not move by one pixel across a
54-second turn, while a comment, a PR body and a ticked checkbox all said they
did.

**The verification was the real failure.** Diffing whole canvases across
snapshots cannot separate *the object turns* from *the highlights travel* — a
rotating rim alone makes every snapshot differ. The right test samples the
specular pixels, and did, once it was pointed at the right claim.

**The physics was inverted too.** For a mirror the reflected sky is world-fixed
and the silhouette turns through it; this had the sky rotating with the body and
the suns nailed to the page. Fixed by counter-rotating the body and sheen, and
by taking each highlight's distance from the boundary in its direction — so a
sun's reflection draws out along the tail as the point sweeps past. Measured
with drift disabled, the specular centroid's radius runs 23, 20, 14, 20, 25.

## 2. The body was opaque and cold on a page that goes red — fixed

`--heat` is published on every route and `.era-wash` glows behind `<main>`, so a
droplet painted in fixed hex blocked it and stayed blue-black through a Chaotic
Era. `SystemCanvas` has carried a comment about exactly this since it was
written, and the rule lived in the file that already obeyed it — so the second
canvas in the repo was written without it.

`src/lib/canvas-ground.ts` now holds the table, an interpolator and a `--heat`
reader, with both canvases importing them. **This is the piece the sophon
inherited**: it warms with the page because the droplet's review made the rule
shared rather than local.

## 3–9, in brief

- **No `<h1>`** — the page had no document outline. Kept in the sophon version.
- **`${color}66` required 6-digit hex** on a shared module whose second caller
  does not exist yet, and would have thrown *inside the clipped section*,
  escaping with the clip and `lighter` compositing still set — silent corruption
  of every later draw. Colours are parsed numerically; the clipped section is
  `try`/`finally`.
- **The cost measurement** measured the integrator, not the page. Narrowed above.
- **The `HEAD` comment inverted head and tail** — `0.42` is the head's radius, so
  the object is 84% head. Anyone tuning it for a small cursor droplet would have
  moved it the wrong way. Worth reading before #10 starts.
- **The tip's rim was shaved** four times a turn at 0.86 of its box.
- **The page was a screen plus a footer**, so it always shipped a scrollbar.
  The 68svh that replaced it was a floor rather than a cap and fixed nothing
  — see finding 2 below.
- **A 404 pasted into Slack** unfurled over the site's sales pitch, because the
  root layout sets the OG and Twitter descriptions absolutely and metadata merges
  per key. Both overridden, and kept.

# Review findings — 2026-09-11

Ten findings against the sophon. Two of them were checked boxes in the section
above, which is the second review running where the claims did not survive
being driven.

## 1. The sophon *was* a cold disc on a red page — fixed

The box said the body took its colour from `--heat` so it would not be one. It
was one.

This canvas is **transparent** — `clearRect`, no opaque fill — and it lives
inside `<main>` at `z-10`, *above* `.era-wash`. `--void` is only half of what a
visitor sees at heat; the wash is the other half and the dominant red. Filling
the interior with `rgba(ground, 0.9)` knocked nine tenths of the wash out and
replaced it with a colour that had none of it:

```
page behind canvas   --void rgb(27,10,10) + wash rgba(226,96,78,~0.15)  ~rgb(57,23,20)
sphere face          rgba(27,10,10,0.9) over that                       ~rgb(30,11,11)
```

**The premise was wrong, and stated twice in comments.** An opaque canvas has
to repaint the page; this one is not opaque and never needed to. `SystemCanvas`
does the *other* half of the rule as well — it redraws both wash gradients
itself — and `canvas-ground.ts` extracted only the flat table, so the sophon
inherited the half that did not move.

**Resolution: the interior is drawn additively.** A surface catching almost no
light is a lightening of what is behind it, not a repaint of it — and
lightening is correct at every heat for free, so the module never has to know
what the page is doing. Measured at full unfold, the canvas now adds
`rgb(179, 204, 230)` at **alpha 10/255** inside the sphere and `[0,0,0,0]`
outside it.

It also deleted work: `SophonCanvas` no longer reads `--heat` at all, so the
`getComputedStyle` four times a second is gone, and `heatFromDocument` went
with it — it had no callers left, which is this PR's own standard.

## 2. `min-h-[68svh]` is a floor, and the page still scrolled — fixed

`min-h` only ever *grew* the block on tall screens; the content was a fixed
~615px whatever the viewport did. Overflow measured at 137px on 1366x640 and
126px on 375x667. It fit the one size it had been checked at.

The canvas and the vertical rhythm are now sized from the viewport, and
**measured with the canvas at a real size** — which matters, because the first
attempt used a bare `min(13rem, 26svh)` and this pane reports a zero-width
document, so `svh` resolved to 0, the canvas collapsed to nothing, and the page
"fit" because the largest thing on it had vanished. `clamp()` with a floor
keeps an object on the page whatever the unit does.

```
1280x800  canvas 240px  overflow 0
1366x640  canvas 192px  overflow 0
375x667   canvas 173px  overflow 0
```

There is a floor: at 1024x500 — 176px of nav and footer against a 180px text
block — 51px still overflows. Recorded rather than chased; below about 560px of
viewport height something has to give, and shrinking the copy is worse.

## 3 and 9. The metadata — fixed

The per-key insight was right and applied to one key. Replacing `openGraph` and
`twitter` wholesale dropped `og:type` (which the spec requires), `og:url`,
`og:site_name` and `og:locale`, and silently downgraded the Twitter card to
`summary`. Every field is restated now, `url` relative so `metadataBase` still
owns the origin, the export is typed `Metadata`, and `robots: { index: false }`
settles the two contradictory robots tags — verified in the built HTML rather
than in the source.

## 4. The meridians were not in the latitudes' projection — fixed

`ctx.ellipse` with a constant rotation is a different projection: it sent every
meridian through a point 0.147R from where the latitude rings converge — about
17px on the 256px canvas — and leaned the whole fan one way, where a globe's
meridians are mirror-symmetric. Traced parametrically now, in the same
orthographic projection the latitudes use, which is four lines and no fudge
factor.

## 5. The cheap page ran 60fps to redraw a dot — fixed

For 22.5 of every 46 seconds `unfoldAt` returns exactly 0 and the canvas holds
a 1px dot, and every frame of it was bit-identical work on the route whose
ticket said *keep the page cheap*.

`unfoldAt` is pure in `t`, so the quiet stretch is known in closed form: the
folded frame is painted once and then slept through with a `setTimeout`, and
frames resume just before it opens. That answers the wake-up question for this
canvas rather than deferring it to a profiler.

## 6, 7, 8, 10. The comments, and an unused option — fixed

- Two comments pointed at `DropletCanvas`, which this branch deletes. Both
  repointed.
- The cycle proportions were wrong in the one place the PR calls *most of the
  design*: fully open is 26% of the cycle and on screen at all is 51%, not
  "about a fifth" and not "two thirds".
- `TURN_SECONDS` named the etching and meant the wireframe; the 0.6 that
  separates them is now `ETCH_PARALLAX`, named and explained as deliberate.
- `SophonOptions.alpha` had no caller and is gone.

## Found while fixing, not in the review

**The canvas could be born invisible.** It measured its box once in an effect
and listened only for `resize`, so a box that was 0 at that moment — which is
exactly what an `svh`-sized element is in a context where `svh` resolves to 0 —
painted into a 1x1 backing store and never recovered. A `ResizeObserver` covers
that and the window-resize case together.

This is the same shape as the review's own point about verification: the bug
was invisible precisely *because* the thing that would have shown it was the
thing that had failed.

## On the review's closing note

Taken. *"Not verified by driving"* was used as a blanket where it covered one
thing. Scroll overflow, the built `<head>` and the composite against the wash
were all checkable here, and all three were wrong. The distinction worth
keeping is between *this needs animation frames* — which this pane genuinely
cannot give — and *this needs a browser*, which is nearly everything else.
