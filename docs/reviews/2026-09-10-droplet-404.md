# A droplet alone in the void — 2026-09-10

**Scope:** #11, on `main...droplet-404`. The page that isn't there.
**Harness:** unchanged at 43/43 — this touches no simulation code.

The smallest ticket on the list, and three of its premises had gone stale.

## What the ticket assumed

**`水滴` was no longer in the font subset.** The ticket says *"`水` is already in
`CJK_GLYPHS` — no font-subset change needed."* `水` was; **`滴` was not** — it
was one of the twenty orphans #12 removed when `check:glyphs` was repaired.

Because #12 also made that check *enforce*, this was a red build rather than a
silent fallback to whatever CJK font the visitor happens to have:

```
1 glyph(s) used but missing from CJK_GLYPHS: 滴   exit 1
```

Which is the repair paying for itself two days later, on the first page to need
a glyph the subset had dropped. `滴` is back; the subset is 78.

**There was no droplet renderer to reuse.** The ticket says it *"reuses the
droplet renderer from the cursor ticket"* and to *"share the droplet draw helper
from `SystemCanvas.tsx` — extract it if it is still inline."* There is no
droplet anywhere in `src`: `SystemCanvas` draws suns, worlds and trails and
nothing else, and #10 has not been built.

So the dependency runs the other way. **This ticket authors the renderer and #10
consumes it** — which is also the easier order, since getting the shape right on
a page where nothing else moves is simpler than getting it right under a moving
pointer.

**The 404 already runs the simulation, and always did.** See below.

## The renderer

`src/lib/droplet.ts`, shared because #10 is the second caller — the same reason
`era-label.ts` moved out of `Hero` a day earlier.

It takes light *directions* rather than sun positions, so a caller with three
real suns can hand it their angles and a caller with none can invent three. It
imports nothing from the simulation and knows nothing about it.

What rotates and what does not is the whole of it, and the first version had
it backwards on both counts — see finding 1. A mirror's reflected sky is
world-fixed and the silhouette turns through it, so the body and sheen are
drawn under a counter-rotation. A specular highlight keeps the direction of
its light, which is why a rotating chrome ball looks static; what moves is how
far out it sits, taken from the boundary in that direction, so a sun's
reflection draws out along the tail as the point sweeps past it.

## The page

`src/app/not-found.tsx` is new. The copy is server-rendered and only the canvas
is a client island, so `/_not-found` still builds `○ (Static)`.

**Composition changed once, on looking at it.** Laid over the copy — the
ticket's "centred" read literally — the three highlights land on whatever
sentence is beneath them, and neither the object nor the text survives it. The
droplet now sits above the words in its own square. Both are legible and the
droplet is still the first thing on the page.

## The open question: the suns drift — decided twice

First answer: **they do not drift**, because the droplet's rotation already
sweeps the highlights across it. That argument was false, and the review below
found why — the highlight transform cancelled the rotation exactly, so nothing
swept by so much as a pixel.

Second answer, taken after the bug was fixed rather than inherited from before
it: **they drift.** Once the transform is right, a mirror's specular highlight
still *stays* in the direction of its light however the mirror turns — that is
what a curved mirror does, and why a rotating chrome ball looks static. What
rotation actually moves is how far out the reflection sits, and on a head that
is mostly circular that is a small effect for most of a turn. So the lights
wander, at three unequal rates, and the page has motion the whole time rather
than once per sun per turn.

Reduced motion still costs no second code path: it renders one frame at t = 0,
so both the rotation and the drift are simply never advanced.

## The constraint that could not be met, and what the number does and does not settle

The ticket says the 404 *"needs a droplet and three notional light positions,
not `EraProvider` and a running three-body integrator. Keep the page cheap."*

`EraProvider` wraps `{children}` in the **root layout**, so it wraps
`/_not-found` exactly as it wraps every route, and its frame loop advances the
integrator whether or not a canvas has registered — deliberately, since the
counter has to keep counting and `--heat` has to keep driving the palette on
`/about` too. Nothing this page does can change that.

So it was measured instead. One second of simulation, with nothing drawing it:

```
5.40 ms per second of wall clock   (median of 12)
0.090 ms per frame                 against a 16.7 ms budget
0.54% of one core
```

**Accepted, and the constraint amended** — but on narrower grounds than this
first said, per finding 5. What that figure covers is the integrator's own
cost, on one machine, with nothing drawing it: it excludes the per-frame
`--heat` write and the style invalidation that follows, and it was taken
before this change added a second animation loop of its own.

The alternatives are still worse — a route group outside the provider costs
the 404 its heat wash and its footer counter and splits the layout for one
page, and skipping integration when nothing draws would stop the counter
advancing on `/about`, which is the visitor's own history and the thing #9
measured the rate of. But *keep the page cheap* on a page nobody stays on is
about wake-ups and battery rather than CPU share, and no figure here settles
that. What was done instead of arguing it: the droplet's loop stops on
`visibilitychange`, so a hidden 404 wakes the main thread for neither loop.
The wake-up question is open, and wants a profiler on the route rather than a
timer around the integrator.

## Not verified by driving

**Rotation and drift under real `requestAnimationFrame`.** The preview pane
reports `document.hidden` and delivers no animation frames, so every motion
test here ran with a timer standing in for rAF. The rAF version is the same
lines with a different scheduler.

Naming this as the risk last time was the mistake finding 1 punishes: the
scheduler was never in doubt and the transform was, and the harness already in
hand could have caught it by sampling one bright pixel instead of hashing the
whole canvas. It now does. What is left genuinely needs eyes — whether three
wandering suns on a slowly turning mirror reads as alive or as restless.

**The wake-up cost of a second animation loop.** See finding 5. Bounded by
stopping on `visibilitychange`, not measured.

# Review findings — 2026-09-10

Nine findings. The critical one falsified this document's own headline claim,
and the verification that was supposed to cover it could not have.

## 1. The highlights did not move. The rotation cancelled itself — fixed

```ts
const local = light.angle - rotation; // inside a ctx already rotated by rotation
```

Composing the two gives `light.angle` exactly. Verified numerically — the screen
offset for one light across five rotations:

```
rot 0.00 -> screen offset 0.360534 -0.454330
rot 0.50 -> screen offset 0.360534 -0.454330
rot 1.70 -> screen offset 0.360534 -0.454330
rot 3.14 -> screen offset 0.360534 -0.454330
rot 5.00 -> screen offset 0.360534 -0.454330
```

Identical to six decimals. **The three suns did not move by one pixel across a
54-second turn**, which falsifies the claim in the file header, the claim in the
PR body, and a checked box in the section above.

The verification was the problem. Diffing whole canvases across snapshots
cannot separate *the object turns* from *the highlights travel*, and the
rotating rim alone made every snapshot differ. The right test samples the
specular pixels, which is what the fix is verified with below.

**Resolution, and the physics was inverted too.** For a mirror the reflected
sky should be world-fixed and the silhouette should turn through it; the first
version had the sky rotating with the body and the suns nailed to the page —
backwards on both counts.

  - The body and sheen are drawn under a **counter-rotation**, so the reflected
    world holds still.
  - A highlight keeps the direction of its light, which is correct for a curved
    mirror. What moves is its **distance from centre**, taken from the boundary
    in that direction: the head is a circle, so most directions sit at `r`, and
    when the tail sweeps past a sun that sun's reflection draws out along it.

Verified by sampling the specular pixels' centroid, **with the drift disabled**
so the movement is rotation's alone:

```
centroid  (126, 99)  (124, 103)  (118, 104)  (118, 89)  (128, 96)
radius        23         20          14         20         25      -> 4 distinct
```

## 2. The body was opaque and cold on a page that goes red — fixed

`--heat` is published on every route, `.era-wash` glows red behind `<main>`, and
an opaque droplet painted in fixed hex blocked it and stayed blue-black. This is
exactly the failure `SystemCanvas` carries a comment about — *"the canvas is
opaque, so it has to repaint the page's own background... these must match
`--void` for the two eras"* — and the rule lived in the file that already obeyed
it, so the second canvas in the repo was written without it.

**Resolution.** `src/lib/canvas-ground.ts` holds `CANVAS_GROUND`, an
interpolator and a `--heat` reader; `SystemCanvas` now imports the table it used
to own, and the droplet takes `ground` as an option. A third canvas gets the
invariant instead of inheriting the bug. Driven: a body pixel goes from
`[22, 28, 38]` at heat 0 to `[52, 43, 55]` at heat 1.

`--heat` is sampled four times a second rather than per frame — it is a
`getComputedStyle` call, and the value is quantised to 1% and falls over
seconds.

## 3. No `<h1>` — fixed

Only paragraphs and a link, so a screen-reader user landing from a dead link got
nothing in the heading list, with the canvas correctly `aria-hidden` and no other
structural signal. `It came in peace.` is the `<h1>`; it loses no styling.

## 4. `${color}66` required 6-digit hex on a shared API — fixed

`DropletLight.color` is a string, and the second caller does not exist yet. A
`rgb(...)` or 3-digit value would throw `SyntaxError` **inside the clipped
section**, escaping with the clip and `globalCompositeOperation: "lighter"`
still set — every later draw on that canvas clipped to a droplet-shaped hole and
composited additively. Silent corruption on the cursor's shared canvas, not a
visible crash.

Colours are parsed to `[r, g, b]` and stops built numerically, an unparseable
colour falls back to the sky, and the clipped section is wrapped in
`try`/`finally` so the context is balanced whatever throws.

## 5. The measurement did not measure what the argument needed — narrowed

The 0.54%-of-a-core figure timed the integrator with nothing drawing it. It
excluded the per-frame `--heat` write and its style invalidation, and it
predates the second rAF loop **this PR adds**. It is also one machine's median,
offered to settle a general constraint on the page most likely to be opened on a
low-end phone from a stale link.

The review's deeper point is the right one: *keep the page cheap* on a page
nobody stays on is about wake-ups and battery, not CPU share, and this change
adds a loop rather than removing one.

**So the claim is narrowed and the loop is made cheaper.** The droplet stops on
`visibilitychange`, as `EraProvider` already does, so a hidden 404 wakes the
main thread for neither. What the number establishes is stated exactly — the
integrator's own cost, on one machine, excluding style work — and the wake-up
question is recorded as open rather than settled by a figure that cannot settle
it.

## 6. The `HEAD` comment inverted head and tail — fixed

`0.42` is the head's *radius* fraction, so the object is 84% head and 16% tail,
not the 42/58 the comment implied. Whoever tunes it for #10's small cursor
droplet would have moved it the wrong way. The comment now says which way it
goes: larger is rounder, not pointier.

## 7. The tip's rim was shaved at the box edges — fixed

At `0.86` of the box the tip reached within 0.25px of the edge against a
1.07px rim, so the brightest thing on the object was clipped every time the
point passed an edge midpoint — four times a turn, reading as the tip
intermittently blunting. `0.82` clears it at every angle.

## 8. The page was a screen plus a footer — fixed

`min-h-[calc(100svh-4rem)]` subtracts the nav but not the ~7rem footer, so a
page built as one still composition always shipped a scrollbar and a droplet
centred in something taller than the window. The value was copied from `Hero`,
where overflowing is the point because there is a page underneath. Now `68svh`.

## 9. The 404 unfurled over the site's sales pitch — fixed

The root layout sets `openGraph.description` and `twitter.description`
absolutely and metadata merges per key, so a dead link pasted into Slack showed
*404 — Kunyuan Hu* over a blurb advertising real-time systems and game
projects. Both are overridden here.

## What this one is worth remembering for

The PR was careful about what it had not verified and named the wrong thing.
Rotation under real `requestAnimationFrame` was three lines with a different
scheduler and was never the risk; the highlight transform was, and the harness
already in hand could have caught it by sampling one bright pixel instead of
hashing the whole canvas. Honesty about coverage is not the same as aiming it
at the right place.
